Startup valuation methods depend heavily on the company's growth stage, ranging from qualitative scoring frameworks for pre-revenue ideas to financial and market-driven models for revenue-generating businesses.

Pre-Revenue and Early-Stage Methods

• Berkus Method: Evaluates a startup across five key success factors (sound idea, prototype, quality management team, strategic relationships, and product rollout or sales). 
Each factor can add up to $500,000, capping the pre-money valuation typically around $2.5 million.

• Scorecard Method: Compares your startup to the average pre-money valuation of similar companies in your region and sector. It adjusts that baseline up or down based on weighted categories like team strength (often 30%), 
market opportunity (25%), and product protection (15%), as detailed by Equidam.

• Cost-to-Duplicate Approach: Calculates the exact historical cost to build the physical assets, write the code, and fund research and development from scratch. 
This acts as a floor for the value, though it ignores future potential or brand value.

Revenue-Generating and Growth-Stage Methods

• Market Multiples (Comparable Company Analysis): Looks at recent acquisition prices or public trading multiples (such as Annual Recurring Revenue or ARR multiples) of similar companies in your industry.
• Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Forecasts the startup’s future free cash flows over several years and discounts them back to their present value using a high discount rate that reflects high investment risk.
• Venture Capital (VC) Method: Works backward from a projected exit value (terminal value) several years in the future. It divides that future value by the investor's desired return on investment (ROI), 
then adjusts for expected dilution from future funding rounds.

A startup valuation calculation determines the economic worth of an early-stage company before or after receiving investment, typically split into pre-money valuation (worth before the new funding) 
and post-money valuation (worth after the new funding is added).

The Core Math Equation

• Post-Money Valuation = Pre-Money Valuation + Amount Raised
• Investor Ownership % = Amount Raised ÷ Post-Money Valuation
• Pre-Money Valuation = Post-Money Valuation − Amount Raised
Example: If an investor puts in $2 million for 20% of the company:
• Post-money valuation = $2,000,000 ÷ 0.20 = $10 million
• Pre-money valuation = $10,000,000 − $2,000,000 = $8 million

Common Startup Valuation Methods


1. Early-Stage / Pre-Revenue Methods

• The Berkus Method: Assigns dollar values (up to $500,000 each, capped around $2.5M total) to qualitative milestones like sound idea, prototype, management team, strategic relationships, and product rollout.
• The Scorecard Method: Compares the startup to an average funded startup in the same region and adjusts the baseline value using weighted criteria such as team strength (30%), market size (25%), and product 
(15%) via platforms like Equidam.

• Cost-to-Duplicate: Totals the actual historical expenses and physical/R&D asset fair market values required to rebuild the company from scratch.

2. Growth-Stage / Revenue-Driven Methods

• Market Multiples (Comparable Analysis): Applies industry-standard revenue or ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) multiples (e.g., 2x–3x ARR or similar recent transaction data) derived from comparable companies.
• Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Projects future free cash flows and discounts them back to present value using a high discount rate to account for high startup failure risks.
• Venture Capital (VC) Method: Works backward from a predicted future exit value (terminal value) 5 to 10 years out, divides it by the target investor return multiple (e.g., 5x–10x), and subtracts the projected investment.
